Apple Wallet

Your Phone Can Hold An ID.
That Is Not The Same As Being Ready.

Apple Wallet can now carry a state driver's license or ID in some states, and a Digital ID built from a U.S. passport. Both are real and both are useful. Neither one covers what happens when a family member is detained. Here is the honest line between them.

What Apple Wallet Actually Holds

On an iPhone, under Wallet → + → Driver's License and ID Cards, you'll find two kinds of credential in the same list:

  • A state ID or mobile driver's license (mDL) — issued by your state's DMV. Only participating states appear in the list, and each state sets its own rules for where it counts.
  • Digital ID — built from a valid U.S. passport by following Apple's setup steps to capture the passport in the app.

Both live in Wallet. Both are presented by tapping your phone against a reader that has been set up to request them.

What Apple Says It Is Not

Apple's passport-based Digital ID is not a passport and not a replacement for one. Apple's own guidance is that it cannot be used for international travel or border crossing. Its accepted uses are domestic — TSA checkpoints at participating airports, and some in-person or in-app identity and age checks.

A state mDL is recognized only where that state and the verifying party have agreed to accept it. Outside of that, it is a picture of a credential.

Neither one obligates a federal immigration officer to accept it. If someone tells you a phone ID satisfies an ICE or CBP agent, they are telling you something Apple does not claim.

Two Things Wallet Structurally Cannot Do

1. It only holds credentials its partners issue. Wallet has no format for the documents that actually decide an immigration case — an I-797 notice, an employment authorization card, an asylum receipt, a green card, a visa, a child's birth certificate, a marriage certificate, an attorney's retainer agreement. There is no DMV issuing those to Apple.

2. It is one person, on one phone, present and consenting. A Wallet ID is shown by its owner, holding their own unlocked phone. It does nothing at 2 a.m. when the phone is in a property bag and a spouse is trying to find the A-number, the attorney's number, and the children's birth certificates.

The Part That Matters

Wallet Is For Proving Who You Are. ICE Ready Is For The Day That Isn't Enough.

If you have a U.S. passport and live in a supported state, put your ID in Wallet. It is genuinely convenient and it costs nothing. We are not asking you to choose.

But the people ICE Ready was built for are frequently the people Wallet cannot enroll at all — no U.S. passport, no supported-state credential, and the documents that matter most to them are ones Apple has never had a slot for.
